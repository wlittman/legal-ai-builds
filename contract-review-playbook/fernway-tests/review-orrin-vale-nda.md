# Project Falcon: Orrin Vale Mutual Confidentiality Agreement (cover note)

**Routing:** NDA → nda-review. Applied the NDA playbook, which uses the same positions on both sides. Orrin Vale is evaluating Fernway's platform, so this is effectively sales-side, on their paper.
**Triage: RED as drafted.** Do not sign it as it stands. Three provisions are outside the confidentiality scope: the §6 non-solicit, the §7 residuals clause and the §9.4 portfolio-company restriction. The redline fixes everything else within policy.
**Files:** `test-nda-project-falcon-redline-internal.docx` (tracked changes, with internal comments marking policy status) and `test-nda-project-falcon-redline-external.docx` (same changes, with short counterparty-facing comments).

## Decisions needed from us

1. **§9.4 portfolio-company restriction. OUT OF POLICY; this is a business call.** It's buried under "Relationship of the Parties." For 18 months, Fernway could not deal with any Orrin Vale Portfolio Company except through Orrin Vale. The §1.3 definition reaches any equity *or debt* investment, or any board seat, across all of their funds. It could block sales to existing customers and pipeline, and it contradicts the stated Purpose (deployment to portfolio companies). **Redline:** strike. **Decision:** whether we would accept any version at all. If Orrin Vale's real concern is being cut out of introductions they make, a narrow version could work: limited to companies Orrin Vale actually introduces, no bar on existing customers or pipeline, and 12 months or less. Anything along those lines is outside the playbook and needs the GC plus the CEO and sales lead. **Before we respond:** ask sales to check current customers and pipeline against Orrin Vale's portfolio.

2. **§6 non-solicit. OUT OF POLICY as drafted.** It runs for 24 months, covers no-hire as well as no-solicit, and extends to every Orrin Vale fund through §1.1. **Redline:** strike. **Decision:** whether to give the playbook fallback up front or hold it back. The fallback is 12 months, limited to employees met through the deal, with a carve-out for general solicitations and no no-hire. Commercial counsel can approve that on deals up to $250K a year; anything broader goes to the GC. I'd hold it back. A PE fund will usually trade this away.

3. **§4 term. OUT OF POLICY as drafted.** Survival is 7 years from disclosure, with no trade-secret carve-out. **Redline:** 3 years, plus trade secrets protected for as long as they stay trade secrets. **Decision:** whether to accept a longer tail if they push back. The playbook has no fallback, so anything over 3 years needs GC sign-off. Before deciding, note that a longer tail mostly protects *Fernway's* information (source code and roadmap), and the clause is mutual. Meeting at 5 years is low-risk if it saves a turn.

4. **Scope check: is Project Falcon only a commercial deal?** The Purpose also covers a "strategic transaction," and the draft has a securities-law acknowledgment (§9.5) and lists "investment information." If Orrin Vale might invest in or acquire Fernway, this should be reviewed as a deal NDA (standstill, financing sources, and so on), not under the commercial playbook. Please confirm with the deal owner.

## Handled in the redline (within policy, no decision needed)

- **§2 definition:** changed from "whether or not marked" to marked *or* reasonably understood to be confidential. Oral disclosures stay covered, with no confirmation requirement.
- **§2(d):** added the missing independent-development exclusion. This is a must-have.
- **§7 residuals:** struck. Residuals are rejected on deal NDAs, and this one has no guardrails anyway (any purpose, no IP exclusion, source code not carved out).
- **§8 return/destroy:** 10 days changed to 30, plus a carve-out for backups and legal-hold copies.
- **§10 assignment:** changed from one-way (Fernway needs consent; Orrin Vale assigns freely to affiliates and portfolio companies) to mutual, with consent not unreasonably withheld.
- **§11 notices:** email notice now takes effect on confirmed receipt or the next business day, instead of on sending.
- **Accepted as drafted:** §3 obligations, the 2-year disclosure window and 30-day termination in §4, §5 compelled disclosure, §9.1–9.3 and §9.5–9.7, Delaware law and courts (§12), §13 injunctive relief, and the "competent evidence" standard in §2.

**Approver:** GC for items 1 and 3 and anything beyond the fallback on item 2. Item 1 also needs a business decision from the CEO and sales lead.
