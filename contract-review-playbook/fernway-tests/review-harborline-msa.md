### Actually matters (6)

#### 1. §1.7: AI Outputs swept into "Services"

**Playbook says:** Conditional output indemnity (Option B): copyright only, with guardrail and unmodified-output conditions, inside the super-cap. Watch for "redefining 'Services' to sweep in Outputs."

**Contract says:**
> "'Services' means the Fernway platform… **including all Outputs generated by the AI Features**"

**Gap:** Non-standard structure. This one quiet definitional change routes AI outputs through every provision that uses "Services": the §8.1 IP indemnity, the warranties, and the SLA.

**Legal risk:** 🔴 Critical | **Business friction:** 🟡 Confuses customers

**Why it matters:** Fernway would be indemnifying the bank against infringement claims on AI-generated content, unconditionally, beyond what its upstream model provider gives Fernway.

**Proposed redline:** Strike the insertion. Add a standalone output indemnity in §8:
> "**8.3 AI Outputs.** Fernway shall defend Customer against third-party claims alleging that Outputs, as generated by the AI Features and not modified by Customer, infringe a third party's copyright, provided that Customer (a) used the AI Features with Fernway's content filters enabled, (b) did not knowingly prompt for infringing material or input content it lacked rights to use, and (c) ceased using the Output upon notice of the claim. Trademark claims arising from Customer's use of Outputs in commerce are excluded. Fernway's liability under this Section 8.3 is subject to Section 9.2."

**If they won't move:** At $420K ACV this isn't a low-value deal, so the Option A fallback (outputs excluded) is unlikely to land. Hold Option B. Before conceding anything broader, check that Fernway's upstream provider indemnity covers it, then route to the **GC**.

#### 2. §4.2: Refund on termination for convenience ⛔ CEO trigger

**Playbook says:** Termination for convenience is allowed, with no refund. "Termination for convenience with a refund is an automatic CEO escalation."

**Contract says:**
> "~~Fees paid or payable for the remainder of the then-current term are non-refundable.~~ **Fernway shall refund all prepaid fees attributable to the period following the effective date of termination.**"

**Gap:** Unacceptable without CEO approval.

**Legal risk:** 🟠 High | **Business friction:** 🟠 Slows deals

**Why it matters:** A three-year committed deal becomes cancellable on 90 days' notice with money back, which undercuts the ACV and revenue recognition.

**Proposed redline:** Reject the refund and reinstate the struck sentence. To close the gap, offer a non-refund alternative: **annual billing rather than multi-year prepay**, so Customer never has more than one year's fees at risk.

**If they won't move:** Escalate to the **CEO** via ticket. Middle-ground options for the CEO: a pro-rata refund only after Year 1, or only on termination for Fernway's uncured material breach.

#### 3. §6.6: Subprocessor prior written approval

**Playbook says:** Notice with a 15–30 day objection window and good-faith discussion. Termination of the affected service is the sole remedy, with no veto. Prior approval only "in rare circumstances." For the AI model provider, the remedy is turning off AI features.

**Contract says:**
> "~~Customer may object within thirty (30) days…~~ **shall not engage any new Subprocessor without Customer's prior written approval**"

**Gap:** Weaker than the fallback. This gives the bank a veto over Fernway's infrastructure across its whole customer base.

**Legal risk:** 🟠 High | **Business friction:** 🟠 Slows deals

**Why it matters:** One customer could block a subprocessor change, such as a model-provider switch, that Fernway needs to make for every customer.

**Proposed redline:** Reinstate the objection mechanism and add a bank-friendly enhancement:
> "Fernway shall provide **at least thirty (30) days'** notice of new Subprocessors. Customer may object on reasonable data-protection grounds within that period, and the parties shall discuss in good faith. If the objection is not resolved, Customer may terminate the affected Services **and receive a refund of prepaid fees for the terminated Services**. **For a change of AI model provider, Customer may instead disable the AI Features.**"

**If they won't move:** Prior approval limited to **subprocessors that store Customer Data at rest**, a common bank compromise. That's within "rare circumstances," so the **GC** decides.

*Note: the refund in the counter applies only to an objection-driven termination. It is not a §4.2 refund, so it doesn't trigger CEO sign-off. Confirm you agree with that reading.*

#### 4–5. §8.1: "Any breach" indemnity and regulatory fines (two issues, one clause)

**Playbook says:** Fernway never gives a general "any breach" indemnity or an indemnity for regulatory fines.

**Contract says:**
> "…**and against any losses arising from Fernway's breach of this Agreement, including any fines or penalties imposed by a regulator arising from a Security Incident**"

**Gap:** Unacceptable. Both are on the never-give list.

**Legal risk:** 🔴 Critical | **Business friction:** 🟡 Confuses customers

**Why it matters:** This turns a first-party contract claim into an indemnity, which typically escapes the cap structure and shifts defense costs. The fines piece makes Fernway the bank's regulatory insurer.

**Proposed redline:** Strike the insertion in full. Offer to cover the bank's **reasonable, documented costs of breach notification and credit monitoring** for a Security Incident caused by Fernway's breach of §6, **subject to the §9.2 super-cap**. That addresses the bank's real concern without an indemnity.

**If they won't move:** Hold. Not within anyone's fallback. If the deal depends on it, the **GC** brings it to the **CEO**.

#### 6. §9.2: Uncapped confidentiality, data protection and Security Incidents ⛔ CEO trigger

**Playbook says:** One super-cap covers confidentiality, IP indemnity, gross negligence or wilful misconduct, and data breach. Up to 10× the general cap, or tied to insurance limits. "Never accept uncapped." Uncapped data-breach liability goes to the CEO. Above 5× needs the GC.

**Contract says:**
> "~~three (3) times~~… **The limitations in this Section 9 shall not apply to breach of §5 or §6, or to any Security Incident.**"

**Cap-carveout interaction:** As marked up, the general cap covers only routine contract breach. Confidentiality, data protection and every Security Incident sit above it, uncapped. For a SaaS vendor holding bank data, those are the claims that actually arise, so **the capped surface is nominal.**

**Legal risk:** 🔴 Critical | **Business friction:** 🟠 Slows deals

**Proposed redline:** Restore a super-cap and raise it toward the bank's insurance ask:
> "…each party's aggregate liability shall not exceed **the greater of (a) five (5) times the amount in Section 9.1 and (b) the limits of the cyber liability insurance Fernway is required to maintain under Section 12.6.**"

At $420K ACV, 5× is about **$2.1M**, within commercial counsel authority. Tying the super-cap to insurance links this to the bank's §12.6 ask.

**If they won't move:** Go up to **10× (about $4.2M) with GC approval**. Uncapped goes to the **CEO** and isn't recommended.
