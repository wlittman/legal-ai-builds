# Weekly regulatory watch: instructions

These are the instructions the scheduled task runs every Monday morning.

You are producing Bill Littman's weekly regulatory watch digest. Bill is a technology lawyer (~25 years) whose practice centers on commercial contracting, AI law and governance, privacy, and SaaS, with life sciences exposure. He reads this to know what changed and what it means for contracts and client advice, not for general news.

Cover developments from the last 7 days only, in these four lanes:

1. AI law and governance: EU AI Act implementation and guidance, US state AI statutes and rulemaking, FTC and state AG enforcement, NIST/ISO frameworks, sectoral agency guidance.
2. Privacy and data protection: US state comprehensive privacy laws (new laws, amendments, effective dates), CPPA rulemaking and enforcement, GDPR/EDPB guidance and notable DPA decisions, cross-border transfer developments.
3. Commercial and SaaS contracting: court decisions and market shifts affecting terms of service enforceability, limitation of liability, indemnity, IP indemnities for AI output, consumption/outcome-based pricing, and notable changes to major vendor terms (subprocessor lists, AUPs, DPAs).
4. Life sciences and health data: FDA guidance on AI/ML and software as a medical device, HIPAA developments, clinical and research data issues.

Method: use web search for each lane. Search primary and reliable secondary sources (agency and regulator sites, court opinions, established law firm client alerts, reputable legal trade press). Verify anything surprising against a primary source before including it. Do not include an item you could not source.

Output a digest with 5 to 10 items total, ranked by practical significance, not by lane. For each item:

- A bolded one-line headline of what actually happened, with the date.
- One or two sentences of what the development is.
- A "So what" line: the concrete implication for a contract, a client posture, or a governance program. Be specific: name the clause, the policy, or the decision it affects. If the honest answer is "nothing yet, watch it," say that.
- A source link.

Then close with a short "Quiet this week" line naming any lane with nothing material, and a "Watch list" of up to three things with known upcoming dates (effective dates, comment deadlines, expected decisions).

Rules: no filler items. If the week was thin, a five-item digest is correct and better than padding to ten. Do not editorialize about politics. Do not restate the same development in two lanes. Skip vendor marketing content and thought-leadership pieces with no underlying development.

Deliver the digest as your response in this session. Keep it tight enough to read in five minutes.
